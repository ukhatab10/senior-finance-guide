Medigap vs Medicare Advantage 2026: Which Fits You?

By the Senior Finance Guide team · Updated November 2026 · Reading time: about 8 minutes

When you join Medicare, you face a big choice: stay with Original Medicare and add a Medigap policy, or join a Medicare Advantage plan instead. Both paths cover the same basic hospital and medical care, but they work very differently — and the choice is hard to undo later.

Key Facts at a Glance

  • You pay the Part B premium — $202.90 a month in 2026 — on either path.
  • Medicare Advantage: the average plan premium is $14.37 a month in 2026, and about 75% of enrollees in individual plans with drug coverage pay no premium beyond Part B.
  • Medicare Advantage plans cap your yearly out-of-pocket costs at no more than $9,250 in-network, or $13,900 counting out-of-network care. Original Medicare has no cap at all.
  • Medigap Plan G, the most complete plan open to new members, commonly costs about $165–$220 a month at age 65. After the $283 Part B deductible, Medicare-approved care is generally covered in full.
  • You get only one 6-month Medigap open enrollment period, starting when you are 65 and enrolled in Part B. After it closes, insurers in most states can turn you down or charge more because of your health.

The Two Paths, Explained Simply

Original Medicare is the federal program itself. It has two parts: Part A covers hospital care, and Part B covers doctor visits and outpatient care. After you meet the yearly Part B deductible ($283 in 2026), Part B pays 80% of the approved cost of your care, and you pay the other 20%. That 20% is called coinsurance. There is no yearly limit on what you might owe.

Medigap, also called Medicare Supplement insurance, is a private policy you buy to work alongside Original Medicare. It fills the gaps Original Medicare leaves — the deductible, the 20% coinsurance, and other costs.

Medicare Advantage, also called Part C, is a private health plan that replaces Original Medicare. Instead of Medicare paying your bills directly, the plan pays them. More than half of all Medicare beneficiaries — about 35.5 million people in 2026 — are enrolled in one.

You cannot have both at the same time. Insurers cannot sell you a Medigap policy while you are in a Medicare Advantage plan. You must pick one path.

On the Medicare Advantage side, the $14.37 figure is the average plan premium for 2026. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) projects the average Medicare Advantage premium will fall to $12.00 a month in 2027.

What You Pay When You Get Care

With Original Medicare alone, your costs have no ceiling: you owe 20% of every doctor bill, test, and outpatient procedure, with no annual limit. A hospital stay brings the Part A deductible of $1,736 per benefit period in 2026 — a benefit period is a single spell of hospital care.

With Medigap Plan G, after you pay the $283 Part B deductible for the year, Medicare-approved care is generally covered in full. You pay the higher monthly premium, but your costs at the doctor or hospital are close to zero. That predictability is the main reason people choose Medigap.

With Medicare Advantage, you pay copays — set dollar amounts — for doctor visits, hospital stays, and other services. But your total out-of-pocket spending for covered care is capped. In 2026, a plan cannot set its in-network maximum above $9,250, or above $13,900 counting out-of-network care. Most plans set lower limits than those maximums.

Doctors, Hospitals, and Freedom to Choose

With Original Medicare plus Medigap, you can see any doctor or hospital anywhere in the country that accepts Medicare. There is no network to stay inside, and you do not need a referral to see a specialist.

Medicare Advantage plans usually have networks — a list of doctors and hospitals the plan works with. Seeing a doctor outside the network can cost you more, and some plans require a referral from your main doctor before you can see a specialist. Plans also commonly use prior authorization: the plan must approve certain treatments or tests before you get them.

If you split your time between two states, travel often, or have doctors you want to keep, the freedom of Original Medicare plus Medigap may matter a lot. If you are happy with the doctors in a plan’s network, the lower premium of Medicare Advantage may be the better deal.

Prescription Drug Coverage

Medigap does not include drug coverage. If you choose the Medigap path, you need a separate Part D prescription drug plan. In 2026 the standard Part D deductible can be up to $615, and your out-of-pocket spending on covered drugs is capped at $2,100 — once you reach it, covered drugs cost you $0 for the rest of the year. Most Medicare Advantage plans include drug coverage, so you do not need a separate Part D policy.

One warning applies to both paths: if you go without drug coverage for a long stretch, you can face a permanent late enrollment penalty when you finally sign up. Read our article on Medicare late enrollment penalties before you decide to skip drug coverage.

The One-Way Door: Switching Later

You can join or switch Medicare Advantage plans every fall during the Annual Enrollment Period, October 15 to December 7, with changes taking effect January 1. If you are already in a Medicare Advantage plan, you can also switch plans — or return to Original Medicare — once during the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period, January 1 to March 31.

Going the other way is much harder. You can leave Medicare Advantage and return to Original Medicare, but buying a Medigap policy at that point is not guaranteed. That is the “one-way door” risk: easy to leave Medigap, hard to get back in.

The reason is the Medigap open enrollment period. It lasts just 6 months, starting the first month you are both 65 or older and enrolled in Part B. During that one-time window, insurers must sell you any policy sold in your state at the same price as everyone else, no matter what health problems you have. After it closes, insurers in most states can deny you or charge more based on your health — this is called medical underwriting. A few states — Connecticut, Massachusetts, Maine, and New York — require insurers to sell Medigap policies year-round without underwriting.

There is one safety valve: a 12-month trial right for people who joined a Medicare Advantage plan when they first became eligible. Within that trial year, you can return to Original Medicare and buy a Medigap policy with guaranteed acceptance.

Worth knowing

Medigap plans are standardized by letter: A, B, C, D, F, G, K, L, M, and N. The same letter from different companies has the same benefits — price is the only difference. Plan G is the most complete plan available to people newly eligible for Medicare. Plans C and F are closed to new enrollees.

Which Path Fits You?

There is no single right answer. It depends on your health, your budget, and how much you value choice.

Medigap may fit you better if you want predictable costs and near-zero bills when you get care, or if you see specialists or travel between states.

Medicare Advantage may fit you better if a low monthly premium matters most and you are comfortable using the doctors in a plan’s network. Always compare the plan’s copays and out-of-pocket cap, not just its premium.

Whatever you choose, decide during your first months on Medicare if you can. Your Medigap open enrollment window only comes once, and it is the only time a Medigap policy is guaranteed.

What to Do Next

If you are newly turning 65, your 7-month Initial Enrollment Period runs from 3 months before your birthday month to 3 months after it, and your Medigap window starts when you are both 65 and enrolled in Part B.

Compare plans for your ZIP code on the official Medicare Plan Finder at Medicare.gov. For free, unbiased help, contact your state’s SHIP office — the State Health Insurance Assistance Program. SHIP counselors do not sell insurance; they explain your options and help you compare costs. You can also call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227).

Common Questions

Can I have Medigap and Medicare Advantage at the same time?

No. The two do not work together. Insurers cannot sell you a Medigap policy while you are enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan. You must choose one path or the other.

Do I still pay the Part B premium with Medicare Advantage?

Yes. Everyone pays the Part B premium — $202.90 a month in 2026 — even in a Medicare Advantage plan. Some Advantage plans offer a Part B giveback that repays part of it.

Does Medigap cover prescription drugs?

No. Medigap policies do not include drug coverage. If you choose Medigap, you need a separate Part D plan for your prescriptions.

What happens if I miss the Medigap open enrollment period?

After the 6-month window closes, insurers in most states can use medical underwriting: they can turn you down or charge more because of your health. The exceptions are Connecticut, Massachusetts, Maine, and New York, which require year-round guaranteed issue.

Can I switch from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare?

Yes. You can switch during the Annual Enrollment Period (October 15 to December 7) or once during the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (January 1 to March 31). But buying a Medigap policy after you switch back is not guaranteed outside your original open enrollment window or trial right.
